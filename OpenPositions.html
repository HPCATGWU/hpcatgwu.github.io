Why HPCAT

The High Performance Computing Architectures and Technologies (HPCAT) Laboratory at The George Washington University conducts research across computer architecture, parallel computing, artificial intelligence (AI) systems, large language models (LLMs), and emerging interconnection technologies. Our work focuses on scalable, energy-efficient, reliable, secure, and sustainable computing systems for emerging AI and high-performance computing workloads.

Current research spans AI/ML accelerators and hardware-software co-design, LLM acceleration and systems optimization, heterogeneous and chiplet-based architectures, Network-on-Chip (NoC) and scale-out interconnection systems, emerging photonic and hybrid interconnect technologies, reliable and resilient computing, and sustainable computing. Our LLM research addresses efficient inference and training, attention and mixture-of-experts (MoE) models, memory and data-movement bottlenecks, and optimization across models, accelerators, and runtime systems.

HPCAT research combines theoretical modeling, architecture and system design, simulation, hardware prototyping, and experimental evaluation. Researchers have opportunities to work across multiple layers of the computing stack and to collaborate with academic and industry partners on emerging computing technologies. 

Postdoctoral Research Associate

Computer Architecture, AI/LLM Accelerators, and Emerging Interconnects

The HPCAT Laboratory invites applications for postdoctoral researchers in computer architecture, AI/LLM accelerators and systems optimization, and emerging interconnection technologies.


The successful candidate will work closely with Professor Ahmed Louri and members of the HPCAT Laboratory on research projects involving next-generation computing architectures and systems. The position provides opportunities to develop independent research directions, collaborate across multiple research projects, publish in leading conferences and journals, mentor graduate students, and contribute to externally funded research programs.


Candidates with expertise in one or more of the following areas are encouraged to apply.


Research Areas

• AI Architecture and Hardware-Software Co-Design.

Architectures and accelerators for AI/ML workloads, including deep neural networks, graph neural networks, and multimodal models; efficient training and inference; compiler-assisted mapping, memory optimization, and adaptive or reconfigurable acceleration.

• LLM Accelerators and Model-Hardware Co-Design.
Specialized architectures for transformer, attention, and mixture-of-experts (MoE) workloads; efficient prefill and decoding; KV-cache and memory-hierarchy optimization; low-precision computation, quantization, and sparsity; and accelerator designs that improve LLM throughput, latency, and energy efficiency.

• LLM Systems and Inference/Training Optimization.
Efficient LLM serving and distributed inference or training; batching and request scheduling; tensor and expert parallelism; MoE routing and load balancing; communication and data-movement reduction; and cross-layer optimization spanning models, compilers, runtimes, accelerators, and interconnects.

• Interconnection Networks and Heterogeneous Computing Systems.
Network-on-Chip and Network-on-Package architectures, chiplet-based systems, heterogeneous CPU/GPU/XPU systems, accelerator-rich architectures, scale-out interconnects, data-center architectures, topology and routing optimization, and communication-aware system design.

• Emerging and Hybrid Interconnect Technologies.
Optical, photonic, electrical, wireless, RF, and hybrid interconnect architectures for multicore, manycore, chiplet, accelerator, and data-center systems; silicon-photonic communication and computing; reconfigurable and workload-adaptive interconnection systems.

• Reliable, Secure, and Sustainable Computing.
Cross-layer reliability and resilience, fault-aware AI systems, secure computer architectures and interconnects, energy-efficient computing, sustainable AI systems, and architecture-level techniques for reducing energy, data movement, and system overhead.

• Intelligent Architecture and Runtime Optimization.
Machine-learning-assisted architecture and system design, runtime resource management, workload-aware mapping and scheduling, adaptive communication and computation, and cross-layer optimization of performance, energy, reliability, and scalability.


Responsibilities
The postdoctoral researcher will participate in the planning and execution of independent and collaborative research. Responsibilities may include:

• Developing new computer architectures, interconnection systems, and AI/LLM accelerator designs, together with runtime techniques and cross-layer optimization methods.

• Designing and evaluating efficient LLM inference and training techniques, including memory management, model execution, scheduling, and multi-accelerator communication.

• Building analytical models, architecture and system simulators, and experimental prototypes to evaluate proposed techniques.

• Preparing manuscripts for leading conferences and journals in computer architecture, high-performance computing, AI systems, and related areas.

• Presenting research results at conferences, workshops, project meetings, and other professional venues.

• Collaborating with faculty, postdoctoral researchers, graduate students, and external research partners.
• Assisting with mentoring graduate researchers and coordinating collaborative research activities.


Candidate Profile and Minimum Qualifications
Qualified candidates should have:

• A Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering, Computer Engineering, Computer Science, or a closely related field. The degree must be conferred by the start date of the appointment.
• A strong research background in computer architecture, parallel computing, AI/LLM systems, interconnection networks, or related areas.
• Demonstrated expertise in at least one of the research areas listed above.
• A record of research publications in peer-reviewed conferences or journals in computer architecture, high-performance computing, AI systems, VLSI/CAD, networking, or closely related fields.
• Experience with architecture or system modeling, simulation, performance evaluation, or hardware prototyping.
• Ability to conduct research independently while collaborating effectively with students, faculty, and external partners. 


Preferred Experience
Experience in one or more of the following areas is desirable:

• Architecture and system simulators such as gem5, BookSim, SST, GPU or accelerator simulators, or comparable research tools.
• RTL design, FPGA prototyping, ASIC design flows, or hardware performance modeling.
• AI/ML frameworks, LLM workload characterization, model execution profiling, and performance evaluation.
• LLM training or inference frameworks (e.g., PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, vLLM, or comparable tools), and optimization techniques for attention, KV-cache management, quantization, or MoE workloads.
• Network-on-Chip, chiplet, heterogeneous computing, or data-center interconnect simulation.
• Photonic or hybrid optical/electrical computing and communication systems.
• Reliability, fault modeling, security, or sustainable computing. 


Apply for Position
Interested candidates should contact Professor Ahmed Louri at louri@gwu.edu and provide:

• A current curriculum vitae.
• A brief research statement describing prior work and current research interests.
• Two or three representative publications.

Applicants are encouraged to briefly indicate which of the research areas above most closely align with their expertise and interests.
